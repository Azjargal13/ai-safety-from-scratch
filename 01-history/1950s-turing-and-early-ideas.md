# Alan Turing’s 1951 Warning

One of the earliest clear statements about the possible risks of advanced machine intelligence came from **Alan Turing** in 1951.

---

## The Quote

In a lecture titled **“Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory”** (given in Manchester in 1951), Turing said:

> “It seems probable that once the machine thinking method had started, it would not take long to outstrip our feeble powers. There would be no question of the machines dying, and they would be able to converse with each other to sharpen their wits. At some stage therefore we should have to expect the machines to take control, in the way that is mentioned in Samuel Butler’s *Erewhon*.”

---

## Context

- Turing was already one of the most important figures in the early history of computing and artificial intelligence.
- In 1950 he had published the famous paper *Computing Machinery and Intelligence*, which introduced the Turing Test.
- By 1951 he was thinking further ahead — about what happens *after* machines can think.

He took the possibility of machines becoming more intelligent than humans seriously, and noted that once that process started, humans might not remain in control.

---

## Why this quote matters

This is one of the earliest recorded moments where a leading scientist clearly stated that advanced machine intelligence could eventually take control from humans.

It shows that concern about loss of control is not a new idea invented in the 2010s or 2020s. The basic worry has existed almost as long as the idea of artificial intelligence itself.

---

## Related early ideas

- Samuel Butler’s novel *Erewhon* (1872) already explored the idea of machines evolving and potentially dominating humans. Turing explicitly referenced it.
- Later thinkers such as I.J. Good (1965) developed the idea of an “intelligence explosion.”

---

**Source**:  
Alan Turing, “Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory” (1951 lecture).  
The text is available in various collections of Turing’s writings.
