# Early Warnings — Primary Sources

Original words from the people who first saw the problem.  
Each entry: the quote, the source, and the safety idea it anticipated.

---

## Alan Turing (1951)

> “It seems probable that once the machine thinking method had started, it would not take long to outstrip our feeble powers… At some stage therefore we should have to expect the machines to take control.”

**Source:** *Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory* (lecture, 1951)  
**Anticipated:** loss of control — see [Turing’s 1951 Warning](../01-history/1950s-turing-and-early-ideas.md)

---

## Norbert Wiener (1960)

> “If we use, to achieve our purposes, a mechanical agency with whose operation we cannot efficiently interfere once we have started it… we had better be quite sure that the purpose put into the machine is the purpose which we really desire.”

**Source:** *Some Moral and Technical Consequences of Automation*, Science (1960)  
**Anticipated:** outer alignment and the off-switch problem — decades before the terms existed

---

## I. J. Good (1965)

> “The first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make, provided that the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control.”

**Source:** *Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine* (1965)  
**Anticipated:** the intelligence explosion — and, in the second half, corrigibility
