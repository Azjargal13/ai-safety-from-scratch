# Learning Paths

This page gives recommended ways to learn AI Safety, depending on how much time you have and what you want to focus on.

All paths assume you start as a beginner.

---

## Path 1: Quick Orientation (3–7 days)

**Goal**: Understand the basic problem and the current landscape.

1. Read these pages in order:
   - [Why AI Safety Matters](./why-ai-safety-matters.md)
   - [Timeline of AI Safety Ideas](./timeline.md)
   - [Turing’s 1951 Warning](../01-history/1950s-turing-and-early-ideas.md)

2. Watch or read one short introduction:
   - Robert Miles – “Intro to AI Safety” (YouTube)
   - Or the short version of Stuart Russell’s talks

3. Skim the “Who’s Who” section below to get a sense of the main people and organizations.

---

## Path 2: Solid Foundation (3–6 weeks)

**Goal**: Build real understanding of the core ideas.

1. Complete Path 1.

2. Read a good introductory book:
   - *Human Compatible* by Stuart Russell (strongly recommended) - I wrote the short summary in [section 7](../07-learning-and-practice/stuart-russell-book-summary.md)

3. Go through one structured free course:
   - **BlueDot AI Safety Fundamentals** (best overall starting course)
   - Or Google DeepMind’s AGI Safety short course

4. Learn the main technical concepts (use the `02-core-concepts/` folder):
   - Outer alignment vs Inner alignment
   - Scalable oversight
   - Interpretability
   - AI Control
   - Evaluations

5. Read a few key papers or summaries (listed in `05-key-papers-and-resources/`).

---

## Path 3: Technical Track (ongoing)

**Goal**: Develop the ability to understand and eventually contribute to technical work.

After completing Path 2:

- Learn practical skills: Python, PyTorch/Hugging Face, running evaluations
- Study mechanistic interpretability (Neel Nanda’s tutorials are a good start)
- Practice simple experiments (see `07-learning-and-practice/`)
- Read technical papers from Anthropic, Redwood, METR, ARC, etc.
- Follow the Alignment Forum and key researchers

---

## Path 4: Governance & Policy Track

**Goal**: Understand the societal and policy side.

- Read the International AI Safety Report (Bengio et al.)
- Read the Singapore Consensus on AI Safety Research Priorities
- Follow work from GovAI, CSET, FLI, and national AI Safety Institutes
- Study current lab safety frameworks (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework)

## Who’s Who — Key People and Organizations

Here is a simplified map of some of the most important figures and groups in AI Safety (as of 2026):

### Early Thinkers

- **Alan Turing** (1951) — Early warning about machines taking control
- **I.J. Good** — Intelligence explosion idea
- **Nick Bostrom** — Existential risk framing
- **Eliezer Yudkowsky** — Founded MIRI, early technical alignment ideas, LessWrong community

### Major Researchers & Voices

- **Stuart Russell** (UC Berkeley, CHAI) — *Human Compatible*, “we grow AI systems” analogy, provably beneficial AI
- **[Yoshua Bengio](https://yoshuabengio.org/en)** — Chair of the International AI Safety Report
- **Paul Christiano** — Foundational ideas on scalable oversight and iterated amplification (influenced ARC and many labs)
- **Geoffrey Hinton** — Public warnings after leaving Google

### Frontier Labs (with major safety efforts)

- **Anthropic** (Dario Amodei and team) — Constitutional AI, Responsible Scaling Policy, strong interpretability work
- **OpenAI** — RLHF, Preparedness Framework, misalignment reporting
- **Google DeepMind** — Frontier Safety Framework, scalable oversight, interpretability

### Important Independent Organizations

- **ARC** (Alignment Research Center) → **METR** (evaluations)
- **Redwood Research** — AI Control
- **Apollo Research** — Detecting deceptive/scheming behavior
- **Center for AI Safety (CAIS)**
- **MIRI** (earlier foundational work)
- **CHAI** (Center for Human-Compatible AI – Stuart Russell)

### Geography

- Strongest concentration: **San Francisco Bay Area** (USA)
- Strong presence: **United Kingdom** (DeepMind, UK AI Security Institute, academic groups)
- Growing activity: **China** (academic + company research)
- Other hubs: Canada (Mila / Bengio), Europe, Singapore (international coordination)

---

## Suggested Next Step

If you are just starting, begin with **Path 1**, then move to **Path 2**.

You can always switch or combine paths later.
