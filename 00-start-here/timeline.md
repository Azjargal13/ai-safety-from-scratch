# Timeline of AI Safety Ideas

A short overview of how the concern about advanced AI developed over time.  
This is a simplified timeline focused on key moments relevant to AI Safety.

---

### 1950–1951 — Early Warnings

- **1950**: Alan Turing publishes *Computing Machinery and Intelligence* and introduces the Turing Test.
- **1951**: Turing gives the lecture *Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory* and warns that once machines start thinking, they may eventually take control.

---

### 1960s–1990s — Theoretical Foundations

- **1965**: I.J. Good writes about an “intelligence explosion” — the idea that a machine smarter than humans could improve itself and rapidly become far more capable.
- Early discussions of control and value alignment begin in academic circles, but the topic remains relatively niche.

---

### 2000–2014 — Modern Foundations

- **2000**: Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) is founded (originally as SIAI).
- Eliezer Yudkowsky and others develop early technical ideas about AI alignment.
- Nick Bostrom and others write about existential risks from advanced AI.
- Stuart Russell begins emphasizing the problem of control more strongly in the AI research community.

---

### 2015–2022 — The Field Grows

- OpenAI, DeepMind, and later Anthropic are founded or expand safety efforts.
- Important concepts such as outer alignment, inner alignment, and scalable oversight are developed.
- Stuart Russell publishes *Human Compatible* (2019), arguing that the current way of building AI is fundamentally flawed.
- More researchers and organizations start treating AI Safety as a serious technical field.

---

### 2023–2026 — Mainstream Attention & Current Era

- Major labs release powerful models and publish safety frameworks (Responsible Scaling Policies, Preparedness Frameworks, etc.).
- International efforts appear, including the International AI Safety Report (chaired by Yoshua Bengio) and the Singapore Consensus.
- Independent evaluation organizations (METR, Apollo Research, etc.) gain importance.
- Growing public and policy discussion about risks, evaluations, and possible regulation.
- As of October 2026, the field is active but many core technical problems remain unsolved.
