# Scalable Oversight

Scalable oversight is one of the central unsolved problems in AI Safety.

---

## The core problem

As AI systems become more capable than humans at many tasks, it becomes harder for humans to supervise them effectively.

**Scalable oversight** asks:

> How can weaker supervisors (humans, or weaker AIs) reliably oversee and correct stronger AIs?

If we cannot solve this, then once models become significantly smarter than us, we may no longer be able to tell whether they are doing what we want or quietly pursuing other goals.

---

## Why simple supervision stops working

Today we can still check most AI outputs:

- We can read the answer
- We can run the code
- We can verify facts

But this breaks down when:

- The AI is working on problems too complex for humans to fully understand
- The AI produces very long chains of reasoning
- The AI is operating in domains where humans have no expertise
- The AI might be deceptive

At that point, “just look at the output” is no longer enough.

---

## Main approaches people are trying

### 1. AI-assisted oversight

Use other AI systems to help humans supervise.

Examples:

- Debate (two AIs argue for opposite sides, human judges)
- Recursive reward modeling
- Critiques and summaries generated by AIs

### 2. Process supervision

Instead of only rewarding the final answer, supervise the step-by-step reasoning process.

### 3. Weak-to-strong generalization

Train a strong model using labels from a weaker supervisor, and try to make the strong model generalize beyond the weak supervisor’s abilities.

### 4. Sandwiching experiments

Test whether weaker systems can successfully oversee stronger ones in controlled settings.

---

## Current status (as of late 2026)

- Some progress has been made with debate, critique models, and process supervision.
- Weak-to-strong generalization is an active research area.
- Most researchers believe we do **not** yet have a reliable solution that will scale to systems much smarter than humans.
- Scalable oversight is widely seen as one of the highest-priority technical problems.
